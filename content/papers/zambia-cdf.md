We present initial results from a randomized evaluation that varies the degree of
decentralization within Zambia's Constituency Development Fund, which has expanded
twenty-fold since 2021. In partnership with the government, we randomly assigned
districts to a reform with two components: reducing central government influence over
local community project selections, and expanding districts' discretion to use
light-touch procurement processes. District officials internalized both reforms,
obtaining project approvals substantially faster and shifting hundreds of tenders into
simpler procurement processes. Increased discretion modestly improves the sectoral
alignment of selected projects with local demand while, at a minimum, not exacerbating
existing biases in the spatial allocation of resources. Reduced bidding on tenders
appears to reflect a reduction in non-competitive bids rather than a consequential
reduction in competition. Early results also suggest this can speed up project
completion without negative effects on quality.
